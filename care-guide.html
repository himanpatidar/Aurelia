Care

How to keep jewelry bright

Anti-tarnish barriers do most of the work. These habits handle the rest — and they matter more than any polish or cleaning product you can buy.

1. Last on, first off

Put jewelry on after perfume, lotion and hairspray have dried. Take it off before cleaning, gardening or swimming in a heavily chlorinated pool. This single habit prevents most damage we see.

2. Dry it, don't just remove it

Water left sitting in a clasp or a bezel is where trouble starts. Wipe pieces with the supplied cloth before putting them away — thirty seconds, not a ritual.

3. Store it dry and separate

Air and humidity cause tarnish; pieces rubbing together cause scratches that break the barrier. A closed pouch or a lined box fixes both.

By material

Rhodium-sealed sterling silver

Safe for showers, sweat and swimming pools. Wipe dry afterwards. Never use silver dip, silver polish or a polishing cloth impregnated with abrasive — those are designed to strip tarnish off bare silver and they will thin the rhodium layer instead. Warm water with a drop of mild soap and a soft cloth is all it ever needs.

PVD-coated surgical steel

The most forgiving thing we make. Shower, swim, exercise, sleep in it. Seawater and chlorine are fine. Clean it with soap and water whenever it looks dull; the finish is bonded to the metal, so you cannot rub it off with normal handling.

18K gold-plated brass

Water-safe but not indestructible. The gold layer is 1.5 microns, so what wears it is friction, not water — stacked rings grinding against each other, a bracelet against a desk edge. Rotate which pieces you wear daily and the plating lasts years rather than months.

Rose gold plating

Treat as gold-plated brass, with one addition: rose gold's warm tone comes from copper in the alloy, and acidic skin can shift the tone slightly over time. Wiping after wear is more important here than on yellow gold.

Pearls and soft stones

Freshwater pearls and moonstone are porous and softer than metal. Keep them away from perfume, hairspray and prolonged water. Never soak them. Wipe with a slightly damp cloth, then a dry one. Our Celeste studs and Astra pendant are the two pieces this applies to.

Storage that actually works

  • Close the container. An open dish on a dresser exposes jewelry to air all day. A zip pouch or a box with a lid halves the tarnish rate on anything unsealed.
  • Add a desiccant. The silica gel sachets that come in packaging are genuinely useful — one in your jewelry box, replaced every few months, keeps humidity down.
  • Separate the pieces. Individual pouches or a compartment box. Chains especially: a herringbone stored loose with rings will get kinked.
  • Keep it out of the bathroom. The single worst place in a home — high humidity, hairspray, cleaning products. A bedroom drawer is better in every way.
  • Hang long chains. Coiled chains tangle and kink. A hook or a chain bar prevents both.

Cleaning, step by step

  1. Warm — not hot — water with a single drop of unscented mild soap.
  2. Soak for two minutes. Skip soaking entirely for pearls, moonstone and glued settings.
  3. Brush gently with a soft baby toothbrush, paying attention to the back of settings where residue collects.
  4. Rinse in clean water.
  5. Pat dry with a lint-free cloth, then leave to air-dry fully before storing. Trapped moisture is the thing you are trying to avoid.

Never do these

  • Toothpaste. It is an abrasive. It will visibly scratch plating. The internet is wrong about this one.
  • Silver dip or tarnish remover. Chemically strips plated finishes.
  • Baking soda or salt pastes. Same problem — abrasive.
  • Ultrasonic cleaners. Fine for solid metal, bad for glued settings, pearls and soft stones.
  • Hand sanitiser. High-alcohol gels dull plating fast, and most of us use them several times a day without thinking about the rings we are wearing.

The 30-second routine

  1. Take the piece off before bed
  2. Wipe it with the cloth
  3. Put it in its pouch, closed

Do this and you will very likely never need anything else on this page.
