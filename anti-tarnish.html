The technology

What anti-tarnish actually means

Tarnish is a chemical reaction, not dirt. Understanding that one fact tells you everything about which jewelry lasts and which does not — and it makes most marketing claims easy to see through.

Why jewelry goes dark

Silver does not rust. It tarnishes, which is a different reaction: sulphur compounds in the air bond with the silver on the surface to form silver sulphide, a black compound. That thin black layer is what you see as dullness, then greyness, then the dark film that a polishing cloth removes.

Three things accelerate it, and all three are unavoidable in ordinary life:

  • Air. Hydrogen sulphide is present in trace amounts everywhere, more so in cities and near traffic.
  • Sweat. Human perspiration contains sulphur-bearing amino acids, plus salt and acids that strip protective layers.
  • Humidity. Water is the medium the reaction needs. Above roughly 60% relative humidity, tarnishing speeds up sharply — which is why a monsoon ruins jewelry faster than a whole dry winter.

Gold-plated brass fails differently. The gold itself does not tarnish, but it is a thin layer over brass, and brass contains copper. Once the plating wears through — usually first at contact points and inside curves — copper oxidises and you get the familiar coppery-green cast, plus the green mark on skin.

The only real fix is a barrier

You cannot make silver stop reacting with sulphur. What you can do is put something between the two. That is all any anti-tarnish treatment is: a barrier layer thick enough and unbroken enough to keep air and moisture off the base metal.

Barriers differ enormously in quality, though, and this is where most jewelry quietly cuts corners. Electroplating leaves microscopic pores. If nothing seals them, air reaches the metal underneath through the pores and the piece tarnishes under its own plating — which looks like dark patches you cannot polish off, because the tarnish is not on the surface.

Every Aurelia piece gets two layers: a metal barrier chosen for the base material, and a clear seal over the top that closes the pores.

In short

  • Tarnish is a sulphur reaction, not surface dirt
  • A barrier stops the reaction; polishing only removes the result
  • Unsealed plating tarnishes from underneath
  • We use two layers on every piece

Being straight with you

What anti-tarnish cannot do

Any brand that tells you its jewelry is permanently tarnish-proof at these prices is overselling. A barrier is physical, and physical things wear. Here is what we will not claim:

  • It is not scratch-proof. A deep scratch breaks the barrier, and tarnish will start at that point. This is the most common real-world failure.
  • It is not chemical-proof. Chlorine bleach, hair dye, nail polish remover and abrasive toothpaste will damage any plating. Toothpaste "cleaning hacks" are the fastest way to ruin a plated piece.
  • It is not forever. Rhodium and gold layers wear thin over years, fastest on rings and bracelets that rub against things. Re-plating restores them.
  • Pearls and porous stones still need care. The metal may be water-rated, but a moonstone or freshwater pearl is not. We flag these on the product page.

What we do claim is specific and testable: with normal daily wear and the care in our guide, a sealed piece stays bright for the length of its guarantee. If it does not, we replace it.

The guarantee, in plain terms

If the finish on your piece tarnishes, discolours or wears through under normal wear within the guarantee window — 12 months on silver and gold-plated brass, 24 months on PVD steel, lifetime on unplated brushed steel — send us a photo and we replace the piece. No polishing-cloth receipts, no proving you stored it correctly.

Not covered: physical damage, scratches from impact, exposure to bleach or solvents, chlorine on pieces not rated for it, and loss. Full terms on the shipping & returns page.
